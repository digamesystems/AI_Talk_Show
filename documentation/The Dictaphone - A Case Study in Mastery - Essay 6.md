I was a senior in high school when my good friend Craig decided he was going to try to get into West Point. He was smart, athletic and of excellent character. The thing was, he needed a letter of recommendation. 

We went to see my father. Standing in his office, we approached him in the afternoon and made the pitch. 

Dad was an attorney and knew pretty much everyone in Tucson and across the state. "A letter of recommendation? Of course, Craig! I'd be happy to!" Dad knew Craig well and this was really no surprise. What was a surprise was when he spun his chair around, grabbed a Dictaphone and dictated a five-paragraph letter of recommendation for Craig *off the top of his head*. "Dear Senator Goldwater..." No outline, no false starts, no revisions. A fully-formed, well-crafted letter, straight from his head onto the tape. 

Craig and I turned to each other in disbelief. "How did he do that?!"

"There! How about I take you guys to lunch? My secretary will have it typed up by the time we get back..." 

---

"How did he do that?!" 

Mastery. 

By the time he dictated that letter, my Dad had been practicing law for more than 20 years. What looked to Craig and me like improvisation wasn't improvisation at all. We were seeing the visible end of thousands of hours of writing, arguing, organizing thoughts and making judgments. Most of the work had simply disappeared. For Dad, crafting that letter was like breathing.
